Why they matter

What your accounts are for.

Annual accounts summarise your business’s financial year. Many businesses are required to prepare them, but they are also one of the most useful documents you have.

Meeting your obligations

Depending on how your business is set up, your accounts may need to be filed at Companies House, and they form the basis of the figures you report to HMRC.

Understanding performance

Accounts show your income, costs and profit for the year, and what the business owns and owes at the end of it.

Supporting conversations

Banks, lenders, landlords and prospective partners may ask to see recent accounts before agreeing terms with you.

Before you get in touch

Useful things to talk about.

You don’t need everything ready to start a conversation. But if you can tell us a little about the following, it helps us understand what you need.

Worth discussing

  • How your business is set up: sole trader, partnership or limited company
  • When your financial year ends, and any deadlines you’re aware of
  • How you keep records now, whether in software, spreadsheets or on paper
  • Whether you’re registered for VAT or run a payroll
  • Whether you’re moving from another accountant
  • What you’d like to understand better about your figures

Helpful to have to hand

  • Your most recent accounts, if you have them
  • Your company registration number, if you trade as a limited company
  • Any recent letters from HMRC or Companies House
  • A note of the questions you’d like to ask
